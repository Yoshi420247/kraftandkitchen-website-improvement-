What is Silicone Release Paper?

Silicone release paper is paper coated with a thin layer of silicone. This creates a smooth, non-stick surface that adhesives release from cleanly.

You've probably encountered silicone release paper before—it's the backing on sticker sheets, labels, and vinyl decals. That smooth, shiny surface that lets you peel stickers off cleanly? That's silicone coating.

For Sticker Storage

Silicone release paper works the same way in reverse. Instead of being the backing you throw away, it becomes a reusable storage surface where you can:

  • Stick stickers temporarily
  • Peel them off later without losing adhesive
  • Reuse the same sheet many times

Why Silicone Release Paper Works for Stickers

The silicone coating creates what's called a "low-tack" surface. Here's why that matters:

Stickers Stay Put

The silicone provides just enough grip that stickers don't slide around or fall off. They stay where you put them.

Stickers Release Clean

When you're ready to use a sticker, it peels away easily. The adhesive stays on the sticker, not on the paper.

No Residue Transfer

Unlike regular paper or plastic, silicone doesn't transfer fibers or residue to your stickers. The adhesive surface stays clean.

Reusable Surface

The same sheet can be used over and over. As long as the silicone surface stays smooth and clean, it keeps working.

Creating Sticker Books with Release Paper

Sticker books have become popular for organizing and collecting stickers. Here's how to make one using silicone release paper:

What You Need

  • Double-sided silicone release paper (8.5" x 11" letter size)
  • 3-ring binder
  • 3-hole punch
  • Optional: sheet protectors, dividers

Why Letter Size?

8.5" x 11" is the standard letter size that:

  • Fits standard 3-ring binders
  • Works with standard hole punches
  • Matches common storage systems
  • Is easy to find and store

Why Double-Sided?

Double-sided silicone coating means:

  • Both faces of each page are usable
  • You get twice the storage per sheet
  • Either side can face up when organizing

How to Set Up

  1. Punch holes in your release paper using a standard 3-hole punch
  2. Insert sheets into your binder
  3. Add stickers to both sides of each page
  4. Organize by theme, color, brand, or however you prefer

Pro Tips

  • Don't overfill: Leave room for pages to lay flat
  • Use dividers: Cardstock dividers help organize sections
  • Consider sheet protectors: For especially valuable stickers, add a protective layer
  • Label sections: Use washi tape tabs for easy navigation

Sticker Storage Tips

General Storage

  • Store flat: Keep your sticker book horizontal or standing upright—not at an angle where stickers might slide
  • Avoid heat: High temperatures can affect adhesive. Keep away from direct sunlight and heat sources
  • Control humidity: Excessive moisture can damage stickers over time

Long-Term Storage

For stickers you're storing long-term:

  • Check adhesive periodically (every 6-12 months)
  • Some adhesives naturally degrade over time regardless of storage method
  • Valuable or irreplaceable stickers may do better in sealed, archival storage

Organizing Your Collection

Common organization methods:

  • By theme: Animals, plants, food, etc.
  • By brand: Group same-brand stickers together
  • By color: Rainbow organization for visual appeal
  • By use: Journal stickers, planner stickers, decorative, etc.
  • By size: Large stickers, small stickers, washi strips

Silicone Release Paper vs. Alternatives

vs. Parchment Paper

Parchment paper can work in a pinch, but:

  • Coating is less consistent
  • May transfer fibers to adhesive
  • Less durable for repeated use
  • Can't use both sides

vs. Wax Paper

Wax paper is not recommended:

  • Wax coating can transfer to stickers
  • Not designed for repeated stick/peel cycles
  • Can affect adhesive quality

vs. Plastic Sheet Protectors

Plastic protectors work differently:

  • Stickers may stick too firmly to plastic
  • Can be harder to remove without stretching stickers
  • Better as an outer protective layer than direct storage surface

vs. Original Sticker Backing

Some people save original sticker sheets, but:

  • Takes up more space (keeping whole sheets)
  • Harder to see what you have
  • Mixed sizes and formats
