What is a PTFE Sheet?

PTFE (polytetrafluoroethylene) is the same non-stick material used in Teflon—which is actually a DuPont brand name for PTFE. In the heat press world, "PTFE sheet," "Teflon sheet," and "non-stick cover sheet" all refer to the same type of product.

Most PTFE sheets for heat press use are made from fiberglass fabric coated with PTFE. This construction provides:

  • High heat resistance (up to 500°F/260°C)
  • Non-stick release surface
  • Durability for hundreds of uses
  • Even heat distribution

The fiberglass weave gives the sheet structure and flexibility, while the PTFE coating creates the non-stick surface that protects your platen and projects.

Why Use a Cover Sheet?

A PTFE cover sheet serves as a protective barrier between your upper heat platen and your design. Here's what it protects against:

For HTV (Heat Transfer Vinyl)

  • Prevents vinyl adhesive from migrating to the platen
  • Protects against carrier sheet adhesive transfer
  • Keeps platen clean for consistent results

For Sublimation

  • Catches ink bleed that goes beyond substrate edges
  • Prevents "ghosting" where ink transfers to platen and marks future projects
  • Essential for sublimation work

For DTF (Direct to Film)

  • Protects against adhesive powder transfer
  • Prevents film residue buildup
  • Maintains platen condition

Without a cover sheet, residue builds up on your platen over time, affecting heat distribution and potentially marking future projects.

Choosing the Right Size

The most common PTFE cover sheet sizes and their uses:

Sheet Size Fits These Presses Notes
16" x 20" 15x15, 16x20 presses Most common size. Fits standard home/small business presses.
18" x 20" Large 16x20 and 18x20 presses Extra width for larger platens or extra coverage.
15" x 15" 15x15 square presses Exact fit for square platens.
12" x 15" Compact/hobby presses For smaller entry-level presses.

Sizing Tips

  • Go slightly larger than your platen - overhang catches edge bleed
  • Match your most common project size - sheet should fully cover working area
  • Have multiple sizes if you work on various project dimensions

3 mil vs 4 mil: Which Thickness?

PTFE cover sheets typically come in two thicknesses:

3 mil (0.003") 4 mil (0.004")
Feel More flexible, easier to handle Stiffer, more substantial
Durability Good—hundreds of uses Better—longer lifespan
Heat Transfer Slightly more efficient Minimal practical difference
Best For Home crafters, standard use Heavy production, commercial shops

Our recommendation: Start with 3 mil. It works for the vast majority of heat press applications. Move to 4 mil if you're doing high-volume production and want extended sheet lifespan.

Temperature and Press Settings

PTFE cover sheets are rated for continuous use up to 500°F (260°C). This exceeds all standard heat press applications:

  • HTV vinyl: typically 305-320°F
  • Sublimation: typically 380-400°F
  • DTF transfers: typically 300-325°F

Do Cover Sheets Affect Settings?

Yes, slightly. The cover sheet creates a thin barrier that can affect heat transfer. In practice:

  • You may need to add 2-3 seconds to your press time
  • Some fast processes may require minor adjustments
  • Always test with your specific materials and settings

Follow your heat press manufacturer's guidelines as your baseline, then adjust based on your results.

Step-by-Step

  1. Preheat your press to the required temperature
  2. Position your garment on the lower platen
  3. Place your design on the garment
  4. Lay the PTFE sheet over the design, covering the entire working area
  5. Close and press for your specified time
  6. Open and remove the cover sheet
  7. Complete your process (peel carrier, etc.)

For Best Results

  • Ensure the cover sheet lays flat with no wrinkles
  • Center the sheet over your design
  • Use a clean sheet—check for debris before each use

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Using a Creased Sheet

Creases can transfer marks to your project. Once creased, a cover sheet should be replaced. Store sheets flat—never fold them.

Mistake 2: Not Cleaning Between Uses

Residue buildup affects performance. Wipe your sheet while still warm after each session.

Mistake 3: Using Damaged Sheets

Tears, holes, or rough spots compromise protection. Inspect sheets regularly and replace when damaged.

Mistake 4: Wrong Size

Undersized sheets don't protect edges. The sheet should fully cover your working area with slight overhang.

Mistake 5: Expecting Identical Results Without Testing

Cover sheets can slightly affect press time and finish. Always test with your specific materials.

Care and Replacement

Cleaning

  • Wipe with a damp cloth while still warm
  • For stubborn residue, use a plastic scraper (never metal)
  • Some crafters use a magic eraser for light cleaning
  • Do not machine wash or use harsh chemicals

Storage

  • Store flat—never fold or roll
  • Hang on a hook or lay flat between uses
  • Keep away from sharp objects

When to Replace

Replace your PTFE cover sheet when:

  • It becomes creased or wrinkled
  • You see tears or holes
  • Buildup won't come off
  • The surface feels rough or textured

With proper care, PTFE cover sheets last hundreds of uses.
