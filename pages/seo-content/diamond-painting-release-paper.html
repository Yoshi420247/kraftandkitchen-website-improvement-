The Problem Release Paper Solves

When you're working on a diamond painting, you can only place diamonds in one section at a time. But here's the challenge:

  • You need to expose the adhesive to place diamonds
  • Exposed adhesive attracts dust, pet hair, and debris
  • The plastic cover that came with your canvas gets cut away as you work
  • Once exposed, there's nothing protecting the sticky areas you're not working on

Contamination on the adhesive means diamonds won't stick properly. It can ruin sections of your project or require touch-up work later.

The Solution: Release Paper

Silicone release paper sticks lightly to the adhesive canvas (enough to stay in place) but peels away cleanly without damaging the sticky surface. You can cover sections you're not working on, protecting them from dust while you focus on one area at a time.

Why 4x4 Squares?

The diamond painting community has settled on 4" x 4" squares as the go-to format. Here's why this size works:

Large Enough to Cover Meaningful Sections

A 4x4 square covers a good working area—about 15-20 diamond rows depending on your canvas. This is enough to protect substantial portions while still being manageable.

Small Enough to Position Precisely

Unlike full sheets of paper, small squares can be positioned exactly where you need them. You can work around completed sections, cover odd shapes, and adjust as your working area changes.

Easy to Move and Reposition

As you complete sections, you simply slide or move the squares to cover the next area. No cutting, no fumbling with large sheets.

Industry Standard

Major diamond painting suppliers like Diamond Art Club use 4x4 squares. Craft retailers like Hobby Lobby stock 4x4 packs. When you buy this size, you're getting what the community uses and recommends.

What About 4.5x4.5?

Some painters prefer slightly larger squares (4.5" x 4.5") for extra coverage and overlap. Both sizes work—it comes down to preference. The extra half-inch gives you more margin, which some find helpful.

How to Use Release Paper for Diamond Painting

Step 1: Set Up Your Workspace

Start with a clean, flat surface. Dust and debris on your workspace will end up on your canvas, so a clean area is your first line of defense.

Step 2: Plan Your Working Section

Decide which area you'll work on. Many diamond painters work in small sections (a few rows at a time) rather than exposing large areas.

Step 3: Peel Back the Protective Film

Remove only the portion of the original plastic cover over your working section. Keep the rest covered until you're ready to work on it.

Step 4: Cover Exposed Areas

Place release paper squares over any exposed adhesive you're not actively placing diamonds on. Squares can overlap slightly—that's fine.

Step 5: Place Your Diamonds

Work on the exposed section. Take your time—the rest of your canvas is protected.

Step 6: Move and Repeat

When you finish a section, move the release paper squares to cover completed areas (if needed) or to expose new working sections.

Between Sessions

When you stop working, cover all exposed adhesive with release paper squares. This protects your canvas until you return.

Works with Poured Glue Canvases

Most diamond painting canvases today use "poured glue" adhesive—a layer of adhesive poured directly onto the canvas. This creates a consistent sticky surface across the entire design area.

Silicone release paper is specifically designed to work with this type of adhesive:

  • Releases cleanly: The silicone coating doesn't bond with the adhesive
  • Doesn't pull up glue: Unlike some papers that might lift adhesive
  • Protects the surface: Creates a barrier against dust and debris

Diamond Art Club, a major supplier, specifically recommends silicone-coated release papers for protecting poured glue canvases.

Tips from Diamond Painters

Keep Squares Flat

Creased or wrinkled squares don't lay flat against the adhesive. Store your squares flat between uses—in a folder, book, or flat container.

Don't Press Too Hard

Lay squares gently on the adhesive surface. Pressing hard can affect the adhesive's stickiness over time. A light touch is all you need.

Reuse Until Worn

Release paper squares can be reused many times. Replace them when they get creased, dirty, or the surface becomes rough.

Work Clean

Wash your hands before working. Oils from your skin can transfer to the adhesive and affect diamond placement.

Use as a Sorting Surface

Release paper squares also work for sorting loose diamonds. The smooth surface lets you slide and organize drills easily.

Protect Completed Sections Too

Some painters cover completed sections with release paper to protect placed diamonds from dust while working on adjacent areas.

Common Questions

How many squares do I need?

For a typical 12" x 16" canvas: 8-12 squares can cover exposed areas while you work. Buy more for larger canvases or if you like having extras. The 200-pack is what most regular diamond painters buy.

Can I cut my own squares?

Yes. If you have full sheets of silicone release paper, you can cut them to size. Pre-cut squares just save time and ensure consistent sizes.

Is this better than the plastic that came with my canvas?

The original plastic works until you cut it away. Release paper squares let you protect any section at any time—including areas you've already exposed. They work together: use the original cover until you need to work a section, then switch to release paper.

Do stickers lose their stickiness on release paper?

Diamond painting adhesive is different from sticker adhesive, but the principle is similar. Release paper is designed to release adhesives without removing them. Your canvas adhesive should retain its stickiness.

Single-sided or double-sided?

For diamond painting, either works since you're only using one surface at a time. Double-sided gives you a backup if one side gets dirty.
