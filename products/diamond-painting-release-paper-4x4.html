Keep Your Diamond Painting Adhesive Clean

When you're working on a diamond painting, you can only place gems in one section at a time. But the rest of your canvas is exposed—collecting dust, pet hair, and debris that can ruin adhesion.

These 4" x 4" release paper squares solve that problem. Cover the sections you're not working on, then move the squares as you progress across your canvas.

Why 4" x 4"?

This is the standard size used by diamond painting suppliers like Diamond Art Club and sold at craft retailers like Hobby Lobby. It's large enough to cover meaningful sections, small enough to position precisely, and matches what the diamond painting community expects.

How Diamond Painters Use Release Paper

  • Section protection: Cover areas you're not actively working on
  • Work in progress storage: When you stop mid-project, cover exposed adhesive
  • Canvas flattening: Some painters use release paper under weights to flatten curled canvases
  • Drills organization: Use as a sorting surface for loose diamonds

Works with Poured Glue Canvases

Our release paper squares are silicone-coated to work with poured glue (full-drill) canvases. The silicone coating releases cleanly from the adhesive without pulling it up or leaving residue.

How to Use

1

Peel Back Your Canvas Cover

Remove only the section of the protective film where you want to work. Keep the rest covered.

2

Place Release Paper Squares

Cover any exposed adhesive areas you're not currently placing diamonds on. Squares can overlap slightly.

3

Work Your Section

Complete your diamonds in the exposed area. Take your time—the rest of your canvas is protected.

4

Move and Repeat

When you finish a section, move the release paper squares to cover completed areas or expose new working sections.

5

Store Between Sessions

If you stop mid-project, cover all exposed adhesive with release paper squares. This keeps dust and debris out until you return.

Pro Tips from Diamond Painters

  • Keep squares flat: Creased squares don't lay flat and can let debris underneath
  • Work in a clean area: Release paper helps, but a clean workspace is your first defense
  • Don't press too hard: Lay squares gently on adhesive—pressing hard can reduce stickiness over time
  • Reuse until worn: Squares can be reused many times. Replace when they get creased, dirty, or lose their smooth surface
  • Store extras flat: Keep unused squares in a folder or between book pages

Frequently Asked Questions

Does it work for poured glue (full-drill) canvases?

Yes. The silicone coating is designed to release cleanly from poured glue adhesive canvases. It protects the adhesive while you work in sections and releases without damaging the sticky surface.

Is it double-sided?

Yes. Both sides have silicone coating, so you can use either side against your adhesive canvas. This also means you can flip squares over if one side gets dirty.

How many squares do I need?

It depends on your canvas size and working style. For a typical 12" x 16" canvas, 8-12 squares can cover exposed areas while you work. The 100-pack handles 1-2 projects with spares. The 200-pack (our best seller) is what most diamond painters buy—it matches what you'd get from Diamond Art Club or Hobby Lobby.

Can I reuse them?

Yes. Squares can be reused across multiple projects until they get creased, dirty, or the surface becomes rough. Store flat between uses.

Why 4" x 4" and not larger?

4" x 4" has become the industry standard for diamond painting. It's large enough to cover meaningful sections but small enough to position precisely around your working area. Larger sheets are harder to manage and reposition as you move across your canvas.

Is this better than using the original plastic film?

The plastic film that comes with your canvas works for initial protection, but once you cut it away, you can't put it back. Release paper squares let you protect any section at any time—including areas you've already exposed. Many diamond painters use the original film until they need to work a section, then switch to release paper for ongoing protection.
