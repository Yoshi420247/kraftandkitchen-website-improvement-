Why Every Heat Press Needs a Cover Sheet

A PTFE cover sheet is the barrier between your design and your heat press platen. It protects your equipment, prevents transfer mishaps, and ensures consistent results.

What Cover Sheets Protect Against

  • Vinyl residue: HTV adhesive can migrate to your platen without protection
  • Ink ghosting: Sublimation ink can transfer to the platen and ghost onto future projects
  • Adhesive buildup: DTF adhesive residue builds up over time without a barrier
  • Scorching: Direct heat contact can damage delicate materials

Cover Sheet Placement

Place the PTFE cover sheet over your design before closing the press. The cover sheet sits between your project and the upper heat platen.

When to Replace

PTFE cover sheets last hundreds of uses. Replace when:

  • Creased or folded (creases can transfer marks to projects)
  • Torn or damaged
  • Visibly contaminated with buildup that won't wipe off
  • Surface becomes rough or textured

Temperature and Press Settings

PTFE cover sheets are rated for continuous use up to 500°F (260°C). This covers virtually all heat press applications:

  • HTV vinyl: typically 305-320°F
  • Sublimation: typically 380-400°F
  • DTF transfers: typically 300-325°F

Important Notes

  • Follow your press manufacturer settings. Cover sheets protect but don't change your base settings.
  • You may need to add 2-3 seconds to press time when using a cover sheet, as the barrier slightly affects heat transfer. Always test.
  • Avoid direct contact with heating elements. Cover sheets are designed for platen use, not direct element contact.

Does It Affect the Finish?

PTFE cover sheets can impart a slight semi-gloss finish to some materials. This is normal and depends on:

  • The material you're pressing
  • Temperature and pressure settings
  • Whether you want matte or gloss results

For matte finishes: Some crafters use a Kraft paper sheet between the cover sheet and design. Test to find what works for your specific materials.

Results may vary. Always test on scrap material before production.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will this change my press settings?

The cover sheet creates a thin barrier that can slightly affect heat transfer. You may need to add 2-3 seconds to your press time when using a cover sheet. Some fast-process applications may need minor adjustments. Always test with your specific settings and materials.

Is this the same as a Teflon sheet?

PTFE is the same material used in Teflon (which is a DuPont brand name). Our PTFE cover sheets provide the same non-stick properties. "PTFE sheet," "Teflon sheet," and "non-stick cover sheet" all refer to the same type of product in the heat press world.

How many times can I reuse it?

PTFE cover sheets can be reused hundreds of times with proper care. Replace when the sheet becomes creased, torn, or develops buildup that won't wipe off. Store flat to maximize lifespan.

What size should I get?

Match your heat press size. The most common size is 16" x 20" (fits standard 15x15 and 16x20 presses with coverage). For larger commercial presses, choose 18" x 20". The cover sheet should be large enough to fully cover your pressing area.

Can I use this for sublimation?

Yes. PTFE cover sheets are essential for sublimation to prevent ink from transferring to your platen. The sheet catches any ink that bleeds past the edge of your substrate and prevents ghosting on future projects.

How do I clean it?

Wipe with a damp cloth while the sheet is still warm. For stubborn residue, use a plastic scraper (never metal). Some crafters use a magic eraser for light cleaning. Do not machine wash or use harsh chemicals.

Storage and Care

  • Store flat: Never fold or crease. Hang on a hook or lay flat between uses.
  • Keep clean: Wipe after each use session to prevent buildup.
  • Avoid sharp objects: Tears and holes compromise protection.
  • Keep multiple sheets: Rotate between sheets to extend overall lifespan.
